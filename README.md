# session2

User profile: 
The final user will be some small business owner or RRHH worker in any company. This person needs to see easily which are my studies, working experience and main skills. 
They might be expecting knowledge and experience related to the job position I might be applying for. 

Information architecture: 
To distribute the content of my cv I have looked for different examples on the internet to begin with. Referenced below.
Afther that, I have started designing with Figma a document, with two different parts, two halfs. One darker and the other brighter. 
To create some contrast visually, and make the information in each part differ clearly. 
When it comes to the content, i wanted to be very visual. I need the reader to see at first: picture, name section, and Working Experience. And all of the other section titles. So they have a clear sight of where to look for each kind of information about me. When I say name section, I mean name but also my contacting information and what I amb pursuing: being a software engineer soon. 
The part where I list some skills I might have on programming and knowledge about certain platforms, I think a simple text sometimes is the best way to show it. If I were to present a CV in some area where only 4-5 skills are really important, maybe I would put like horizontal bars with my level on them. But computer science is much more than 4 topics, and I see it better this way. 
References are not in my real CV yet, but this is probably a great information to put if you've already worked elsewhere in the job position you apply for. 


Visual design: 
The color palette is already explained and represented in the Figma document. But to summarize I've used: dark blue, white and black. 
And the typography is Inter, which I've had to adjust weight (in css) and boldness (in figma) to get to see a similar font-style in both formats, both titles and regular texts. Also, to add 'Inter' font family into the Css, I needed to use a link in the html file (top part) I found in W3Schools. 
For the css part, I needed to distribute the sections 'div' both in vertical, in general, but then have common properties when some right and left sections were the same height or begun in the same point. To do that, you will many elements have multiple classes, so then in the css I can apply some general and then some specific styles to different elements. 
Also I thought of making it pretty basic, not from a boredom point of view, but from a minimalistic and easy to see everyting sense. As anyone would hate reading something with lots of text, or unorganized sections, with no space between them (to breath). And also people like basic colors, one or two, and not much contrast, so that's what I tried doing.
